Activity 2: Reading Comprehension

World War I: A Conflict That Reshaped the World

World War I, fought between 1914 and 1918, was a conflict that transformed nations, societies, and the lives of millions. What began with the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo soon escalated into a global war: alliances pulled countries into the struggle, and diplomatic tensions exploded into open combat. The nations of Europe marched toward war with confidence; many leaders believed the conflict would end quickly. Instead, it became a catastrophe of unprecedented scale.

Along the Western Front, soldiers lived in trenches that stretched like scars across the landscape. Mud clung to boots like iron chains, and the roar of artillery echoed endlessly through the air. The battlefield was a furnace of destruction; every attack seemed to feed the flames of suffering. Through vivid imagery, contemporary accounts describe shattered trees reaching toward the sky like skeletal fingers and fields transformed into barren wastelands. Such scenes revealed the terrible cost of modern warfare.

Technology changed the nature of combat. Machine guns, poison gas, tanks, and heavy artillery increased the deadliness of every engagement. Soldiers faced dangers from every direction: bullets rained from above, shells burst without warning, and toxic clouds drifted silently across the ground. Hyperbole appeared in many wartime letters, as soldiers described bombardments that seemed powerful enough to shake the entire world. Although exaggerated, these expressions captured the fear and exhaustion experienced by those on the front lines.

The war was also a battle of ideas and emotions. Governments used propaganda to inspire patriotism and maintain public support. Posters portrayed enemies as monsters; newspapers emphasized victories while downplaying defeats. Meanwhile, families at home endured uncertainty, grief, and economic hardship. The conflict became a metaphorical storm that darkened an entire generation. Hope flickered like a candle in the wind, yet many people continued to believe that sacrifice would eventually bring peace.

When the war finally ended in 1918, the consequences were immense. Empires collapsed, borders changed, and millions mourned the loss of loved ones. The Treaty of Versailles sought to establish peace; however, it also created tensions that would influence future events. World War I was more than a military struggle: it was a turning point in human history. Its legacy remains a powerful reminder of the costs of conflict and the importance of international cooperation.

Activity 3: The Colon

Explanation & Uses

The colon (:) acts as a gateway, signaling to the reader that what comes next will explain, illustrate, or elaborate upon the preceding independent clause.

  • Introducing Lists: The committee requires three things: dedication, innovation, and teamwork.
  • Introducing Explanations/Elaborations: He had only one objective in mind: he wanted to win the gold medal.
  • Introducing Quotations: The CEO began the meeting with a stark warning: "We must adapt or perish."
  • Emphasis: After hours of searching, we finally found the missing item: the TV remote.
  • Salutations in Formal Letters: To Whom It May Concern:
  • Time and Ratios: The movie starts at 7:30 PM. The ratio of students to teachers is 20:1.

Note: The text preceding the colon must form a complete sentence (independent clause), except for salutations, time, or ratios.

Activity 4: The Semicolon

Explanation & Uses

The semicolon (;) represents a pause that is longer than a comma but shorter than a full stop. It is used to enhance sentence variety and demonstrate close conceptual relationships.

  • Linking Independent Clauses: Use it to join two closely related independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction (and, but, or).
    The economy experienced a severe downturn; nevertheless, the tech sector continued to thrive.
  • Linking Clauses with a Transitional Phrase:
    The weather was terrible; therefore, we decided to cancel the picnic.
  • Complex Lists: Use it to separate items in a series when the items themselves contain commas.
    The delegates arrived from London, England; Paris, France; and Berlin, Germany.
